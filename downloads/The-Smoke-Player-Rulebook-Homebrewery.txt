# Welcome to the ends

{{kicker A London crime roleplaying game}}

You live in the gap between the rent and the next payday. Your phone is cracked, your trainers have seen better days, and Auntie Pat wants two bicycles before tonight. The arches on Morning Lane smell of chain oil and somebody else's trouble. If you can get the locks off and bring the bikes back, there is money in it. If somebody recognises their own frame, there is a story in it too.

*The Smoke* is a game about small jobs that become big obligations. Start in Hackney as a nobody in The Chainring. Work a shift, recover a disputed motor, carry somebody's sound system across London, blag your way into a private room, or follow the story behind a missing recording. Your crew chooses its clients and decides what success is worth.

### Around the table
One person is the **GM**, who describes London, plays its people and presents the consequences. Everyone else plays a **player character**, or PC. Say what you want and how you try it; the GM calls for a roll when the outcome is uncertain and a failure would matter. Everyone helps keep the fictional situation clear.

You need this book, a character record, pencils or a shared digital record, and d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12 dice. The d20 appears in the highest Rep tier. Bring several d6s for combat speed dice. Foundry VTT can keep the records and make the rolls, but the procedures in this book also work at a table.

\column

### What play looks like
**A lead.** Somebody needs something: a fare, a recovery, a quiet word, a piece of evidence. Agree the fee and the deadline before the crew commits.

**A plan.** Choose your approach, the people you trust, your route and the tools you can actually bring. A good introduction can do more than a better gun.

**A risk.** Roll when it matters. Your result tells you whether the job works and what it costs. Police attention is Heat; injuries become lost Health and Conditions; promises become future work.

**A handover.** Money changes hands. Somebody accepts the result or disputes it. Record XP, cash and relationships, then show who remembers what the crew just did.

### This book and the campaign book
This volume contains the rules, equipment, vehicles and four ready-to-play characters. The companion campaign book contains the opening, faction careers, local cases, GM truths, cast and venue keys. Players can read all of this volume without learning the campaign's hidden answers.

{{note
**The city is your playing space.** A listed mission is an offer. You can refuse it, negotiate it, take another side or pursue your own lead. The GM makes the response concrete: who knows, what they want, and what happens next.
}}

# Your character record

### The nine stats
Each stat is a die. A bigger die is a stronger stat; each die also has a modifier. Your Effort die normally starts at d6. Rolls, Health and Guard use different parts of the record, so keep the die and its modifier visible.

| Stat | Use |
| :-- | :-- |
| Muscle (MUS) | Lifting, shoving, fighting hand to hand, kicking a door in. |
| Grit (GRT) | Taking a hit, running all night, staying conscious, holding a hose on a fire. |
| Moves (MOV) | Dodging, climbing, vaulting fences, running away, slipping cuffs. |
| Wheels (WHL) | Driving and riding anything. |
| Aim (AIM) | Shooting, throwing, hitting a moving target from a moving vehicle. |
| Graft (GRF) | Locks, hotwiring, pickpocketing, sleight of hand, fixing an engine. |
| Savvy (SAV) | Street smarts: spotting cops and tails, knowing London, first aid, finding a fence. |
| Front (FRT) | Chat, bluff, charm, intimidation, haggling, talking down a copper. |
| Bottle (BTL) | Nerve. Keeping your head when it goes wrong, standing your ground, not grassing. |

\column

### The numbers beside your name
**Health** measures how much harm you can take. At Rep 1, maximum Health is 8 + your Grit modifier. Damage reduces current Health; being patched up restores it up to the maximum.

**Guard** is the fixed target for physical attacks against you. Start with 7 + the better of your Moves or Grit modifier, then add the Guard bonus from your worn armour. Growth can raise the underlying base later. Conditions and cover change the current number.

**Hustle** is your reserve for a crucial roll. Begin with 3 points and a maximum of 3. A safe night's rest restores it. New Rep tiers can increase its maximum.

**Cash** is what is in your pocket. The **stash** is money left at your flat. Hospital losses and custody fines use pocket cash; the stash is separate.

**Rep** is your personal level, reached through XP. **Crew, street and police relationships** are separate counters. **Faction Renown and Influence** belong to your dealings with particular organisations; they are explained later in this book.

**Conditions** are named effects, such as Hurt or Shaken. Write down when each ends. **Kit** records weapons, armour, ammunition, tools and consumables. Mark which armour is worn and how much ammunition remains.

{{note
**Example:** Moves d6 and Grit d4 give an unarmoured Guard of 10: 7 + 3. Grit d4 gives 10 Health: 8 + 2. A Stab Vest adds 1 Guard and reduces incoming damage by 2; those are two separate benefits.
}}

# Making a new face

### Seven steps to your first night
1. **Who are you?** Choose a name, street name, pronouns, age, your ends and your look. Player characters are adults, aged 18 or over.
2. **Choose a background.** The following pages give ten starting points, their suggested stats, starting cash, kit and three perk choices.
3. **Assign your stats.** Use exactly **one d8, three d6s and five d4s** across the nine stats. The background spread is a suggestion; you may rearrange it while keeping that distribution.
4. **Choose one perk** from your background's three. Write its stat and the situation that makes it apply.
5. **Take your kit and cash.** Add your background's items, Fists and one extra from the list below. Record quantities and worn armour.
6. **Give yourself a story.** Write your hustle, vice, bond and ambition. Agree a reason to stick with the other PCs.
7. **Finish the numbers.** Rep 1, 0 XP, 3 Hustle and Health 8 + Grit modifier. Work out Guard and start with no faction Renown unless the GM establishes otherwise.

### One extra thing
Choose a Bike Lock, Balaclava, Spray Paint, Meal Deal, Painkillers, Bandages, Cigarettes, Torch, Oyster Card or Duct Tape. This is one item, rather than a cash allowance.

\column

### Put a person behind the dice
**Your hustle** is what people know you for: no lock stays shut, you know a bloke, or you can drive anything with wheels.

**Your vice** is something that draws you into bad decisions: pride, adrenaline, trainers, buying rounds or the fruit machines. It gives you choices to play; it does not take your character out of your hands.

**Your bond** gives somebody a claim on you. Perhaps Kez taught you to ride, Pat paid for your mum's funeral, or Mo let the rent slide. Decide what you owe and what that person expects.

**Your ambition** tells the GM what a good offer looks like. A legitimate shop, your own crew, one score and out, a sponsorship deal, or a better place for your family all give money a purpose.

### Make a crew
Each player names one PC they have helped and one PC whose judgement they sometimes question. Agree where you sleep, what transport you can actually use, and how you divide a crew fee. No background gives you a vehicle unless it is explicitly supplied for the opening or bought in play.

### Ready-made characters
Jaz Kaur, Danny “Tealeaf” Doyle, Femi Adebayo and Big Sal Moretti appear near the end of this book with their existing stats, possessions and personal stakes. They use the supplied campaign's starting records.

# The roll

### Effort + stat + modifier
Roll your **Effort die**, normally d6, and the appropriate **stat die**. Add both results, the stat modifier and any other modifiers. Both dice normally **explode** on their highest face: keep that result, roll that die again and add it. Keep doing so while the maximum repeats.

| Stat die | d4 | d6 | d8 | d10 | d12 | d20 |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Modifier | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 | +6 | +10 |

Before rolling, agree a **Target Number**, or TN. The GM either uses a printed fixed TN, another character's relevant total, or a difficulty band calibrated to your unboosted dice. Your **margin** is the total minus the TN.

| Margin | Outcome | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| −4 or less | Proper Mess | Fails badly; serious consequence. |
| −3 to −1 | Bad Luck | Fails and costs you. |
| 0 | Just About | Success with a cost. |
| +1 to +3 | Sorted | Clean success. |
| +4 to +7 | Smashed It | Success and an extra benefit. |
| +8 or more | Legendary | An exceptional result. |

### Say the stakes first
The GM states what you can achieve and what failure puts at risk. A lock can cost time, a missed connection can move the meeting, and a loud arrival can create witnesses. A roll does not decide a character's every thought or erase an established fact.

\column

### A lock under the arches
Danny has Graft d8, giving +4. The GM calls the lock Medium: with Effort d6 and Graft d8, the TN is 12. Danny rolls 5 on Effort and 8 on Graft. The 8 explodes; its next roll is 2. Total: 5 + 8 + 2 + 4 = **19**. Margin +7 is **Smashed It**.

The lock opens and the GM offers an extra benefit that fits: the bike is free before the owner looks up. The exploding die is also a **Nice One**. It adds a fitting flourish or opportunity; it does not automatically pay for the same benefit twice.

### Explosions and big moments
Count the explosions across both dice, including repeats from the same die.

| Explosions | Moment |

# Choosing difficulty

The GM chooses a difficulty that matches the situation. Easy aims for about 85% success, Medium 60%, Hard 35% and Long Shot 12%. Whole-number TNs make the actual chances vary a little. The table below uses the normal Effort d6 and normal explosions.

| Stat | Easy | Medium | Hard | Long Shot |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| d4 | 6 (87%) | 8 (62%) | 10 (39%) | 15 (12%) |
| d6 | 8 (83%) | 10 (58%) | 12 (38%) | 17 (13%) |
| d8 | 9 (87%) | 12 (58%) | 14 (39%) | 20 (12%) |
| d10 | 11 (83%) | 14 (58%) | 17 (32%) | 22 (12%) |
| d12 | 12 (86%) | 16 (58%) | 19 (34%) | 24 (13%) |
| d20 | 18 (83%) | 23 (60%) | 28 (36%) | 34 (11%) |

### Set it before the boost
Use the character's **unboosted stat and Effort dice** to set a band-derived TN. Then keep it fixed while applying Hustle, a perk, Handling or a temporary penalty. Recalculating the target after a boost would take away its benefit.

A printed fixed TN stays fixed. So does a target taken from an opponent's roll or Guard. Vehicles replace the normal Effort die with Performance; the driving TN tables later in this book cover those combinations.

\column

### Keep a known obstacle known
Once you have established a specific lock or task and its TN, keep the same target for that attempt and circumstances. Another character can try a different approach, but changing who rolls does not rewrite what the crew already learned about the obstacle.

**Easy:** the right kit, time and a favourable position; roll only if the remaining risk matters.

**Medium:** an uncertain job within the character's competence.

**Hard:** serious pressure or a demanding approach. Describe what makes it hard and what a failure changes.

**Long Shot:** a desperate attempt where help, Hustle or a better plan may be needed.

### Opposed situations
When an action calls for a contest rather than a fixed defence, have the opposing side roll the relevant stat and use its total as the acting character's TN. Describe who is trying to change the situation before rolling. For attacks, use Guard; for a Dodge reaction, use the actual attack total.

### Changing the situation
Tools can lower a lock's difficulty before its TN is set. A strong fictional advantage may remove the need to roll. Circumstantial modifiers, perks and Hustle then alter the roll against the established target. Explain which benefit applies and why; do not silently charge for the same obstacle twice.

# Hustle, perks and your luck

### Spending Hustle
Choose before rolling and pay even if the roll fails.

| Spend | Benefit for this roll |
|:--|:--|
| 1 Hustle | Step the Effort die up once. |
| 2 Hustle | Become **On a Roll**: both dice explode on their highest two faces. |

These are alternative spends. Two Hustle buys On a Roll; it does not also grant the one-point Effort upgrade. On a Roll changes the explosion threshold, so d6 explodes on 5–6 and d8 on 7–8.

### Perks
One relevant perk steps its associated **stat die** up once. Use it when both the stat and the situation fit. Perks do not stack with one another on a roll. Use the modifier of the stepped stat die: d6 becomes d8 and its modifier becomes +4.

### The die ladder
d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 → d20.

Before the Empire tier, a temporary step above d12 gives **+2** instead. Further overflow steps each give another +2. At Empire, temporary steps can reach d20. Permanent increases always follow your current Rep tier's stat cap.

\column

### Off Your Game
Off Your Game prevents **both dice** from exploding on an affected roll. Some Conditions affect particular stats. In Pain is different: it stops **only Effort** from exploding, so the stat die can still explode normally.

If On a Roll and Off Your Game both apply, they cancel to normal explosions for that roll. A separate rule blocking Effort explosions, such as In Pain, still applies. Bonuses and penalties from Conditions still count.

### Before you throw the dice
1. Establish the action, stat and consequences.
2. Fix the target using the unboosted dice, a printed TN or a defence.
3. Replace Effort with vehicle Performance if driving.
4. Choose one relevant perk and a Hustle spend.
5. Apply any die changes, Conditions, Handling and other modifiers.
6. Roll, explode as allowed, total, and read the margin.

{{note
**A better getaway.** Jaz has Wheels d8 and drives a d6-Performance motor. A Hard check starts at TN 14. Getaway Driver changes Wheels to d10 and +5. Spending 1 Hustle changes Performance-as-Effort to d8. The TN stays 14; Handling is added to the result.
}}

# Teamwork and challenges

### One shared attempt
When the whole crew contributes to one agreed action, everyone rolls against the established target. Add their totals, divide by the number of participants, and **round down**. Read that average's margin once. Decide who can meaningfully help before rolling.

For example, totals of 9, 12 and 16 average 12 after rounding down. Against TN 12, the group gets Just About. They accomplish the shared task and pay one fitting cost.

### A job with several stages
For an extended challenge, give the job a progress target and a consequence limit. Each meaningful contribution adds separately according to the table. Before play, say what progress looks like and what reaching the consequence limit means.

| Outcome | Progress | Consequences |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Proper Mess | 0 | 2 |
| Bad Luck | 0 | 1 |
| Bad Luck, cost accepted | 1 | 1 |
| Just About | 1 | 1 |
| Sorted | 1 | 0 |
| Smashed It | 2 | 0 |
| Legendary | 3 | 0 |

\column

### Taking a cost
On Bad Luck, a character can accept a fitting cost for 1 progress and 1 consequence when the challenge allows it. Say what that cost is before the player decides. Accepting it does not turn every failed attack or specialised job procedure into a success.

### Pick one procedure
A shared attempt averages totals. A progress challenge adds contributions. A fire, medical call or pursuit has a specific procedure. Choose the one that matches the scene and use its result once.

### Fiction still matters
A broken part needs a replacement; an exhausted witness needs time; a room may need a sponsor. A progress track records change in the situation. It does not make missing tools, people or permission appear.

### Failure opens the next scene
Show a concrete response. A buyer leaves a forwarding contact. A rival collects the disputed goods. A claimant demands payment. Essential campaign leads remain discoverable; a poor roll changes the cost, timing or position of getting them.

### A first challenge
Getting a borrowed rig across town might need **4 progress before 3 consequences**. A careful route is Savvy; the crowded junction is Wheels; an anxious promoter is Front. Announce the next consequence as it approaches: a late fee, lost position or the client taking another offer.

This is an example for the GM to adapt, rather than a replacement for a mission's named objectives.

# When it kicks off

{{kicker Speed dice combat}}

### Start a round
Everyone rolls a number of **ordinary d6 speed dice**, secretly, then reveals them together. Speed dice do not explode. The number comes from Moves.

| Moves or Performance | Speed dice |
| :-- | :-- |
| d4 | 1d6 |
| d6 or d8 | 2d6 |
| d10 or d12 | 3d6 |
| d20 | 4d6 |

Stunned removes one die and Knackered removes one more, to a minimum of one if you can act. Down, Cuffed or surrendered characters have no speed dice. In a vehicle chase, the driver uses the better of their personal count and the motor's count; a vehicle's count comes from its Performance die.

### Read the queue
Each individual die is **one action**. Arrange all results from highest to lowest across everyone. On a tie, the better Moves die acts first, then a PC before an NPC. If still tied, use alphabetical name order, consistently.

On your die, take one action and an accompanying short move of a few squares where the fiction allows. Vehicle movement uses its gauge instead. Mark that die as used; then resolve the next die in the queue.

\column

### End a round
1. Resolve ongoing harm: Bleeding loses 1 Health and Burning loses 2.
2. Remove Stunned and round-long defensive effects.
3. Count a round without aid for anyone Down. Resolve the Down procedure.
4. Resolve vehicle fires when due and check whether the chase has ended.
5. Begin the new round, restore available reactions and roll fresh speed dice.

### One reaction
You have **one Dodge reaction per round** unless a Condition prevents it. Spend it when an incoming hit is offered, rather than on your own action. The Dodge action is a separate defensive choice that raises Guard.

### Actions and intent
Attack, Aim, Dodge, Take cover, Reload, Grab, Break free, Cuff and Surrender are described on the next pages. A meaningful improvised action also uses a die. Tell the GM what changes if it works; agree a stat and target when a roll is needed.

### With the simple tracker
The alternate core tracker uses exploding d6 + Moves modifier for initiative, one action and one-zone move per turn, and a reaction refreshing at the start of that turn. Bleeding resolves at the end of that actor's turn. Use this structure for the whole encounter if selected; the default procedure in this book is speed dice.

# Attacking and defending

### Make an attack
Choose a weapon and a target in range. Use **Muscle** for hand-to-hand weapons or **Aim** for guns and thrown weapons, following the weapon's entry. Roll against the target's **current Guard**, including cover, armour's Guard bonus and Conditions. A margin of zero or more hits.

### Take the hit or Dodge
The target can accept the hit or spend their available reaction to **Dodge**. Roll Moves against the **attack's total**. Meeting that total succeeds and cancels the whole hit. Failing spends the reaction and the hit lands. Resolve a hit only once.

**Stunned** prevents a reaction. **Down** prevents acting or reacting. These restrictions apply even if you have not spent the reaction yet.

### Damage
**Damage = attack margin + weapon Impact − armour reduction**, minimum 0. The margin is measured against the original Guard, not the Dodge total. Subtract damage from Health, or Body for a vehicle.

Only worn armour provides its listed protection. Wearing a second suit replaces the first; it does not stack another full armour benefit.

\column

### Extra effects
A weapon can inflict a specific effect as well as damage: stun gives Stunned and Prone, gas or smoke gives Gassed, and fire gives Burning. A bleed weapon adds Bleeding on Smashed It or Legendary when it causes damage.

After a damaging hit, the GM may add **one appropriate Condition** from the severity suggestions. The severity is guidance, not a command to apply every listed Condition. **Down is automatic at 0 Health**; a high damage band alone does not set you Down while you still have Health.

### Defensive actions
**Dodge:** spend your action to gain +2 Guard until your next action. This does not spend the Dodge reaction.

**Take cover:** light cover gives +2 Guard. Heavy cover gives +4 Guard and +1 damage reduction. Full concealment blocks ordinary targeted attacks. An area or smoke effect needs the GM to establish what its area actually reaches.

### Example: the bat
Sal attacks Guard 10 with a Cricket Bat, Impact 3. He totals 13: margin +3. The target has 1 armour reduction and no reaction left. Damage is 3 + 3 − 1 = **5**, a Severe injury. The GM chooses Bleeding. If the target had 8 Health, they now have 3 and remain standing.

# Distance, ammunition and holds

| Band | Distance | Use |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Engaged | Up to 1.6 squares | Hand-to-hand and very close fire. |
| Near | Over 1.6 to 10 | Pistols, SMGs, shotguns, throws. |
| Far | Over 10 to 25 | A weapon whose entry reaches Far. |
| Distant | Over 25 | Only a weapon listed for Distant; −1. |

Use the map's squares consistently within a scene; they are play units rather than a promise that every tile depicts the same real distance. A weapon's listed range is its maximum. Engaged includes very close diagonal contact.

### Shooting
**Single shot:** spend one matching round. **Burst:** an eligible weapon spends three rounds for +2 Impact. You need the whole ammunition cost to fire that attack.

**Aim:** spend an action to step Effort up once for your next shot. A relevant Steady Hand perk can separately step Aim up.

**Reload:** spend an action and transfer matching rounds from your carried supply to the magazine. Write down both the magazine and the spare ammunition.

**Drive-by:** a passenger fires at −1. A driver fires at −2 and cannot use a two-handed weapon. Enclosed occupants gain +2 Guard; exposed riders do not. Shooting at tyres takes −2; a successful tyre hit flattens one tyre.

**Close quarters:** shotguns gain +2 Impact at Engaged; rifles take −2 to the attack total there. A weapon allowed at Distant takes −1 when used at that range.

\column

### Grab
At Engaged, roll Muscle against the target's Guard. A success makes them **Grabbed**: they cannot move away, Moves is Off Your Game, and Guard is −2. A grab is a hold, not an extra free damaging attack.

### Break free
Spend an action. Roll Muscle or Moves against the grabber's Guard. On success, remove Grabbed and describe how you escape the hold. Other forms of Restrained may use the holder's appropriate opposing roll or the obstacle's established target.

### Cuff
You can cuff someone who is Grabbed, Down, Restrained, Stunned or surrendered. You need cuffs or a suitable restraining item and must reach them. Cuffed makes Muscle, Moves, Aim and Graft Off Your Game and prevents driving.

### Surrender
Hands up; you take no further speed-die actions and can be cuffed without a roll. Decide what accepting surrender means in the fiction before it becomes a routine button. Police cuffing a PC leads to Busted.

### Get up
Remove Prone by using your movement. If you are also Grabbed, Cuffed or Down, resolve that restriction too; getting up does not free a hold or restore Health.

# Harm, aid and recovery

| Damage | Severity | Suggested Condition |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0 | None | None |
| 1–2 | Minor | Bruised, Shaken or Prone |
| 3–4 | Moderate | Hurt, Stunned or Restrained |
| 5–6 | Severe | Bleeding, In Pain, Broken or Bricking It |
| 7+ | Critical | Broken or Bleeding; Down at 0 Health |

### At zero Health
You are **Down**, with −4 Guard. You cannot act except to roll **Grit, Medium, to Stay Conscious** when called for. A Proper Mess on that roll makes you Wasted. Otherwise, after **three rounds Down without aid**, you are Wasted anyway. Success on Stay Conscious does not heal you or restart the clock.

Someone reaching you with aid can get you back above 0 Health; remove Down and stop its unattended countdown. The first-aid procedure below normally restores 2 Health, and assistance that only stabilises a Down character can return them to 1 as its specific effect states.

### First aid
Reach the patient, or treat yourself. Roll **Savvy, Medium**. On a success, restore **2 Health** and remove **Bleeding and Hurt**. Treat an injury once; a fresh injury allows a fresh attempt. Food, medicine and treatment use their own listed effects.

\column

### Rest
A safe night's rest at your flat or an agreed safehouse restores full Health and Hustle and clears everyday fatigue, pain and stress Conditions. At two Heat stars or less, it clears Heat overnight too.

You cannot rest while Bleeding, while police can see you, or at three or more stars. Deal with those problems first. A long-term injury does not disappear because a short-term rest effect clears the other Conditions.

### Homerton A&E
Treatment at Homerton restores Health and clears the usual treatable injuries. Broken is set, but remains until **a week of recovery** has passed and the GM removes it. Ordinary A&E treatment is free in the game's service list; the **Wasted** procedure has its own pocket-cash loss.

### Wasted
You wake at Homerton A&E. Clear Heat and resolve the hospital reset. Lose **10% of pocket cash, rounded to whole pounds, with a minimum of £50**, capped at the cash you actually have. Your stash is untouched. If you had £30, you lose £30; with £800, you lose £80.

### Keep Conditions readable
Apply their actual penalties and note their removal. Three Bruised marks become Hurt. Effects that ignore a Condition suppress its penalties; they do not erase the injury. Numbed suppresses Hurt and In Pain; when it ends, an untreated Condition can matter again.

# Getting a motor

### Vehicles have their own record
**Performance** replaces Effort on a Wheels roll. Add your Wheels die, its modifier and the vehicle's **Handling**, plus the relevant situational modifiers.

**Speed** is the green movement limit per action in a chase. **Body** is the vehicle's Health. **Armour** reduces damage to Body. **Size** sets vehicle Guard: **6 + twice Size**, before temporary defences. Fuel, seats, value and lock difficulty appear in the roster.

### Seats
A motor has one driver, additional crew positions if listed, then passenger spaces. Board an actual free seat and record who occupies it. Everyone aboard travels with the motor. A stretcher uses patient space; a windowless load bay blocks seeing or attacking out; a cage requires someone outside or in an authorised front seat to release its rider.

### Driving modifiers
Blue lights on suitable emergency vehicles give +1 Wheels. Ordinary vehicles take −2 off road; an Off-road vehicle gains +1 there instead, while Nimble vehicles avoid that penalty. A Tight turning circle gives +1 at a junction. Apply only the abilities the motor actually has.

\column

### Theft and recovery
Roll Graft against the established lock target. A relevant tool may lower its difficulty before that target is set. The equipment chapter gives each tool's game benefit; the fiction stays at the level of a lock attempt, its noise and its consequences.

Sorted or better opens the lock. With a loud tool, Sorted can draw witnesses. Just About opens it but somebody sees. Bad Luck costs time; a loud attempt is seen. Proper Mess is seen and can jam the lock. Decide what the vehicle still needs before it can move: an open door and a running motor are separate facts.

Carjacking uses Muscle or Front and attracts the carjacking Heat response. Do not award extra hidden Heat for an unseen ordinary theft; apply the scene's actual witnesses.

### Plate markers
ANPR reads a vehicle's plate and its recorded marker. A stolen motor passing a patrol risks a hit. New plates or the abstract respray service change that marker in the game. A docking-station hire bike has no resale value to a fence.

### Crossing London
Use connected street exits. Zones describe meaningful roads, blocks and rooms; drawn roads are the route. A campaign journey is not automatically a time penalty for every tile crossed. Use a clock when a job or the scene establishes a deadline.

# The chase gauge

When police get eyes on a wanted driver, or chase a suspect on foot at two stars or more, the immediate map can move into speed-dice rounds. Police units and nearby traffic take actions too.

### How many actions?
Use the speed-count table with a motor's Performance die for an unoccupied police or traffic unit. A PC driver uses the greater of that count and their personal count. Two or more flat tyres remove one vehicle die; a motor at 0 Body removes another, with a minimum of one while it is still a viable participant. A burning or destroyed motor is not free to drive normally.

### Distance belongs to the action
At the start of each driving action, reset distance travelled to zero. Add all movement in that action, including small separate moves. The limits are **cumulative**: red is twice Speed total, rather than green plus yellow plus red.

| Band | Total distance | Check / Handling |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Green | Up to Speed | No gauge check / normal |
| Yellow | Up to round(1½ × Speed) | Medium / −1 |
| Red | Up to round(2 × Speed) | Hard / −2 |

\column

### Make the check at the line
Crossing into yellow triggers its check before you carry on. Reaching red triggers its check and replaces the yellow Handling penalty with −2; the penalties do not add to −3. A success lets you continue up to the current limit. A failure ends that action's driving and applies the loss-of-control result.

For Speed 10, the gauge is green 10, yellow 15 and red 20 squares. If you have already driven 12, the action has 3 yellow squares or 8 red squares left. It does not gain another 20.

### Tyres and damage
Each flat tyre steps Performance down once, reduces Handling by 2, and shortens Speed by 2 squares, to a minimum of 2. Below half Body, round Speed × 0.85 to the nearest whole square. Round yellow at 1½ times adjusted Speed and red at twice adjusted Speed.

### Getting away
At the end of a round, if **every wanted participant is more than 30 squares from every pursuing unit**, the immediate chase ends. Exactly 30 is not enough. Remaining Heat still needs to cool. In the fiction, explain the lost contact; the police response can begin again if they spot you.

# Skids, collisions and contact

### Failed gauge checks
Bad Luck in yellow costs **2 Body**; in red, **3 Body**. A Proper Mess costs **4 Body** in yellow and **7 Body** in red. Size 3 or larger adds 1 Body loss. A Proper Mess also gives Shaken; a red Proper Mess deals 2 damage to riders. Stop the driving action and describe the skid, spin or crash.

### Evasive manoeuvre
When a move crosses another vehicle, make a Wheels check: **Easy in green, Medium in yellow, Hard in red**. Sorted or better swerves clear. Just About scrapes both vehicles for 1 Body each.

For Bad Luck, each vehicle loses **2 + pace + the other vehicle's Size** Body. For Proper Mess, each loses **4 + pace + twice the other vehicle's Size**. Here **pace** is 0 in green, 2 in yellow, 4 in red. Proper Mess deals 2 damage to riders and gives Shaken; ordinary red failure deals 1 to riders, and yellow or red ordinary failures give Bruised.

Resolve movement at the contact point. Proper Mess stops the action. When Body reaches 0, move immediately to the vehicle-fire procedure.

\column

### Go evasive
Spend an action to weave and gain **+2 vehicle Guard** against shots, rams and PITs until the end of the round. This is different from a reaction Dodge by a person.

### Ram
Roll Wheels against the target's Guard. On a hit, collision damage is **margin + twice your vehicle's Size**. Your motor also suffers half that amount, rounded up, when ramming a vehicle; against a person it suffers a quarter, rounded up, and positive damage knocks the person Prone. These are the manoeuvre's direct harm amounts, rather than a second weapon attack.

### PIT
Get alongside within 4 squares. Roll Wheels against the vehicle's Guard. A success forces its driver to make **Wheels, Hard** to hold the line. Failure spins the target and costs its next speed-die action: lose **4 Body**, or **5 Body at Size 3 or larger**, and give riders Shaken. This uses the yellow Proper Mess spin result even though the resistance roll is Hard.

### Keep an incident record
Write who hit whom, Body lost, rider injuries, flat tyres, and any witnesses. A surviving claimant can matter more than a repair bill. Police use these same stakes: at higher Heat they push harder, ram and bring armed response.

# Fire, repairs and selling up

### At zero Body
A vehicle at **0 Body catches fire**. In a round-based scene it explodes at the **end of the next round**; outside it, the game's countdown is about **15 seconds**. Everyone must get out. A destroyed shell cannot be driven or restored with an ordinary roadside patch.

| Where | Harm | Conditions |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Still inside | 10 damage | Burning |
| Outside, within 2 squares | 5 damage | Burning and Prone |
| Over 2 to 4 squares | 2 damage | Prone |
| Vehicle within 2 squares | 5 Body | Fire if reduced to 0 |
| Vehicle over 2 to 4 | 2 Body | Fire if reduced to 0 |

An exploding motor can damage nearby motors too, causing further fires if it takes them to 0 Body. Resolve each threatened vehicle and its occupants; make the countdown visible to everyone affected.

### Repairs
**Patch up:** while in or beside the motor, roll **Graft, Hard**. Success restores 2 Body, or 4 on a strong result. This roadside patch is available once until a garage works on the motor.

A **Toolkit** is its own equipment procedure: a Medium Graft repair restores 2 Body on Sorted, 4 on Smashed It, or all on Legendary, and can fix a flat. Just About succeeds with an agreed cost. It requires a repairable vehicle, time and the kit. Do not apply both repair entries to one roll.

\column

### Garage work
The listed garages charge **£10 per missing Body**. A flat tyre counts as six Body for pricing: £60. Major wrecks and the cost of replacing a shell are matters for the GM and the available parts, not an extra source of infinite roadside healing.

### The fence's offer
Pat's bicycle share is **35%** of base value. Bow Breakers' motor share is **25%**. Calculate:

**Value × share × (0.4 + 0.6 × remaining Body fraction).**

If it is hot, multiply that by **0.8**. Round to the nearest **£5**. At Rep 6, the known-supplier bonus raises the final fence offer by 10% where applicable. Apply one sale and its agreed ownership consequences.

**Example:** a £450 mountain bike at full Body yields £157.50 before rounding, so £160. If hot, the offer is £126 before rounding, so £125. Damage reduces it further.

### Buyers
Bicycles and suitable two-wheelers go to Pat at The Arches; cars and vans go to Bow Breakers Yard. A particular mission may provide another buyer, disputed ownership or an agreed return. Selling the same property to two people creates a claimant, rather than two clean paydays.

# Work the borough

### Start a shift
Get into a suitable vehicle as its driver and choose a shift: taxi, courier, police, paramedic, fire or bus. A callout names the job, destination, deadline and next stage. Accept it or pass. The Chainring's bike orders use the same habit: agree the order, meet it, then hand it over.

Each completed service job normally awards **1 XP**. A completed Pat order awards **2 XP**. Service work is a fictional job system; its uniform, vehicle and access are provided by the GM's scenario, not granted by choosing a background.

### Taxi
Reach the fare, collect them in a free seat, and drive to the named destination. Wheels resolves the demanding part of the journey. Distance and time remaining set the fare and tip; the Cabbie perk can apply. A fare is not completed merely by reaching its pickup.

### Courier
Collect the parcel or order, then reach the delivery point. The state of the cargo and punctuality affect pay. Courier applies to the relevant driving check and its listed late-payment benefit. A damaged or disputed delivery still creates a client with something to say.

\column

### Bus
Follow the stops in order, stopping at the named road zone. Each stop pays for punctuality and finishing the route can add a bonus. The game's route is a scenario, not a real timetable.

### Police
**ANPR sweep:** a marked motor is hidden among lookalikes. Read plates with the police vehicle's scanner, identify the hit and make the stop.

**Pursuit:** the service call can use an abstract gap. Start at 2; close to 0 to catch, or let it open to 5 and the target escapes. A successful check closes by its challenge progress; Bad Luck opens 1; Proper Mess opens 2 and causes a collision. The getaway TN is 4 + half the sum of driver Wheels and vehicle Performance faces + Handling, rounded.

**Disturbance:** reach the scene, establish what is happening and resolve the requested check. An arrest needs a person you can lawfully contain within this fictional job, a suitable seat and delivery to custody.

### Which chase?
The gap procedure belongs to an abstract service pursuit. The mapped police chase uses the v0.5.0 movement gauge. Use one procedure for the immediate pursuit; do not charge both a gap failure and a gauge crash for the same failed roll.

# Patients, fires and job clocks

### Paramedic
Reach the patient and roll **Savvy** for treatment. Their state is **Stable → Fading → Faltering → Passing → Lost**. Stable is best; Lost ends that callout.

Sorted improves one step; Smashed It or Legendary improves two. Just About holds the current state. Bad Luck worsens one; Proper Mess worsens two. An untreated non-Stable patient worsens every **45 seconds of active job-clock time**. Make room in a suitable vehicle before loading. A stretcher bay allows treatment in transit. Deliver to A&E; pay depends on the arrival state and journey.

### Firefighter
Reach the fire and roll **Grit**. Use the challenge contribution table. Extinguish it at **4 progress before 3 consequences**. If both limits are reached on one roll, the consequence limit takes priority: the building is lost. Rescuing and moving people remains a fictional priority alongside the structural track.

### Job time
The digital game counts active clock time and can pause it. At a physical table, the GM records the same windows or announces the equivalent approaching deadlines before they matter. Unrelated conversation need not secretly consume a patient's clock.

\column

# Money, shops and a safe bed

### Buy what is actually available
Ordinary corner shops sell food, water, basic medicine and small essentials. Pat's Arches sell cycle tools and repairs. Markets supply clobber; Uncle's Cafe supplies more dangerous kit when your personal Rep unlocks it. The roster prices are game prices.

A listed service states its charge before the crew accepts. Respray is £100, a market change is £25, and garage Body repair is £10 per missing point by default. The GM may change service prices as an established campaign setting.

### Discounts
Street relationship 5 gives 10% off shops; Chainring relationship 3 gives Pat's 10% discount. Wide Boy gives another 10% where applicable. Total shop discount is capped at **20%**. These relationship counters are distinct from personal Rep and from faction Renown.

### Split the fee
A **crew fee** is one amount for the whole participating crew. Divide it by the number of participants, rounding each share down to whole pounds. A **per-head payment** is paid to each participating PC. Mission entries label them separately.

\column

### Stash and obligations
Move cash between your pocket and the flat's stash when you can reach it. Write down a shared stash's ownership and who may spend it. Busted and Wasted remove pocket cash, not money you have already put away.

A borrowed motor, an equipment booking, a deposit and a property share each need their own terms. Record the owner, weekly costs, what happens if it is damaged, and what work the deal requires. Buying a made-up premises creates an ongoing business with people and claims attached.

### Access is a relationship
Public rooms can be open from the start. Private meetings, staff rooms, a regular rehearsal slot or a workshop bay need a named sponsor, booking or negotiated approach. Renown can earn repeat access; it does not erase a venue's staff, existing tenants or opening arrangements.

# Shift pay and tips

Use **H** for street-map hops and **T** for the fraction of the delivery clock remaining, clamped from 0 to 1. Round money to whole pounds unless a formula says otherwise. The printed mission fee is separate from a service shift or a vehicle sale.

| Work | Calculation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Taxi fare | £12 + £9H, plus £4 if using a cab. |
| Taxi tip | Fare × (outcome tip rate + 0.25T), rounded. Tip rate: Proper Mess / Bad Luck 0; Just About .05; Sorted .12; Smashed It .20; Legendary .35. Add to the fare. |
| Courier | (£8 + £7H) × (0.6 + 0.6T), rounded. Halve the amount for damaged cargo. |

# Armour

Only the armour you wear counts. Its Guard bonus makes you harder to hit; its reduction subtracts from damage after the attack lands. Do not add the protection of several worn suits together.

| Police Body Armour | +2 | 3 | restricted |
| Riot Shield | +3 | 1 | restricted |
| Ballistic Vest | +2 | 3 | £700 |

**Puffer Jacket.** Thick enough to take the edge off a punch.

**Leather Jacket.** Old-school. Takes the edge off a knife and a slide down the tarmac.

**Motorbike Leathers.** Leathers and a lid. Reduction 2, and coming off a bike does half damage.

**Stab Vest.** Covert vest under a hoodie. Police wear the hi-vis version.

**Bulletproof Vest.** Covert body armour. Stops a pistol round; you still feel it.

**Police Body Armour.** Met-pattern body armour.

**Riot Shield.** TSG kit. +3 Guard while you hold it; you cannot use a two-handed weapon.

**Ballistic Vest.** Issued to armed units; conspicuous on the street.

**Heavy Work Jacket.** A thick work coat. Better than a T-shirt when trouble starts.

# Rep, Renown and Influence

### Personal Rep
Earn XP from jobs, missions and the GM. Rep has 33 levels. Meeting a threshold makes the next level available; apply every level in order. XP is cumulative and is not spent when you level.

| Rep | Tier | Permanent cap |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1–4 | Street | d8 |
| 5–10 | Known | d10 |
| 11–18 | Firm | d12 |
| 19–26 | Guv'nor | d12 |
| 27–33 | Empire | d20 |

At an **odd new level**, raise one stat by one step within the new tier's cap. At an **even new level**, choose a new perk. Every **third new level**, roll Growth as well. Entering Rep **5, 11, 19 or 27** adds 1 maximum Hustle.

### Growth
Roll your **Grit die once**, without explosions, for Health. Increase maximum Health by half the result, rounded up, minimum 1. Roll the better of **Moves or Grit once**, without explosions, for Guard: 1–2 gives no increase, 3+ adds 1 to the base, and the die's top face adds 2 instead.

### Advances
The GM can award a separate advance after meaningful play. Spend one to raise a stat one permanent step within your current Rep tier cap. This does not replace a level's benefit or spend XP.

\column

### Relationship counters
The character's **crew, street and police** counters track recognition in those circles. Police relationship 3 makes routine stops start at Wanted. Street 5 and crew 3 unlock the shop discounts described earlier.

### Faction Renown
Each of the twelve campaign organisations keeps its own **Renown** with the crew. It starts at 0 and opens career jobs at 0, 1, 3, 5, 8, 11, 15 and 20. Completing those eight stages awards 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5 and 5 Renown.

| Renown | Rank | Influence cap |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0 | Unproven | 1 |
| 3 | Known | 2 |
| 7 | Trusted | 3 |
| 12 | Connected | 4 |
| 18 | Insider | 5 |
| 25 | Pillar | 6 |

### Influence
Influence buys a defined favour while leaving Renown intact. Career jobs restore 1 Influence, or 2 for a finale, up to the current cap. Typical costs are 1 for an introduction, 2 for agreed access, equipment hire or a lift, and 4 for a major intervention the faction can actually support. Money, staff and capacity still need to exist.

An Open, Allied, Strained or Hostile stance describes the current relationship. Renown records history; it cannot compel a hostile patron to help.

# Playing through Foundry

{{kicker Optional digital table guide}}

### Your sheet
**Identity and stats:** your name, ends, background and nine clickable dice. Clicking a stat opens the roll choices. Choose one applicable perk and any Hustle before rolling.

**Resources:** Health, Hustle and pocket cash. **Kit:** weapons, ammo, worn armour, tools and usable consumables. **Conditions:** check the active names and their effects, then remove a Condition when its recovery happens.

**Rep:** XP, the next threshold, Level Up and any advances the GM has awarded. The Character Creator on a blank sheet builds a new PC through the seven creation steps.

### The Blower
Open your phone from the scene controls or **Alt+B**. It holds callouts, Pat's orders, mission objectives, your money and Heat. A job's **Directions** changes from pickup to delivery as the job progresses. **Alt+N** opens sat nav for other destinations; it follows your own token and route.

### Getting in
Right-click a vehicle, choose **Board**, then select a seat. The driver moves the motor and its occupants. Vehicle sheets show Body, Fuel, Guard, the movement gauge, rollable manoeuvres and an Incidents record. Step into an EXIT strip to follow a linked map edge.

\column

### A fight on screen
Use the Combat Console to roll speed dice privately. The GM reveals the queue. Take one action for the current die and choose Done. An incoming hit offers Take hit or Dodge; the target's owner or GM resolves it once. The GM can then choose one fitting additional Condition.

### Services
**Local services**, **Shop here**, **First aid** and **Rest** appear on your sheet or token where relevant. Reach the patient before first aid. Reach the shop or safehouse before using its services. A vehicle's patch or hotwire action still needs the fictional situation to permit it.

### Radio
The vehicle's driver tunes the car radio for those aboard; each listener controls their own volume. The Blower has a radio on foot. Real live streams provide atmosphere. Fictional Dead Air programmes, clue recordings and campaign phone-ins are played by the GM or supplied as handouts.

The optional studio-message controls open an actual station chat or your own messaging app. A campaign dedication can simply be roleplayed at the table; it does not require contacting a real broadcaster.

### GM controls
The GM Screen handles checks, hazards, rewards and tools. Street Control handles setup and city activity. The Mission Board offers **Story mode**, where the GM starts story missions, and **Self-play**, where players can run open missions. All modes use the same underlying rules and recorded obligations.

# A round at the table

The crew has recovered a flight case. A rival reaches the van outside the venue as the police turn into the street. The GM puts the immediate risks on the table: the case, the driver, the clear route, and two witnesses filming.

### Reveal the dice
Jaz has two personal speed dice, 5 and 2. Sal's Moves d6 gives two, 4 and 1. The rival rolls 5. Jaz's better Moves breaks her tie, so the first action is hers. These are ordinary d6s: nobody explodes a speed die.

### Jaz drives
Her van has Speed 8. She takes the clear road for 8 green squares. The next turn needs 3 more, entering yellow. Before carrying on, she rolls the fixed Medium Wheels target using the van's Performance instead of Effort, with Handling −1. Sorted gets her to square 11 of the action. She has used one speed die; her next die will start a fresh gauge.

### The rival swings
The rival totals 12 against Sal's Guard 10 with a Bike Chain, Impact 2. The attack would deal 4 before armour. Sal spends his reaction and rolls Moves against **12**. A total of 12 is Just About and avoids the hit entirely. Sal has no reaction until the next round.

\column

### Sal makes space
On his 4, Sal grabs the rival at Engaged. Muscle against Guard succeeds. The rival is Grabbed and Guard falls by 2. Sal moves with the hold only as the GM agrees the fiction allows; grabbing somebody does not grant an extra attack or unlimited movement.

### The police arrive
The GM determines who actually saw the assault and whether the arriving officers witnessed it. A witnessed assault adds 1 Heat, plus 1 if police saw it. The crew does not receive the gunfire Heat entry because nobody fired a gun.

### Finish the round
After the remaining actions, the GM resolves ongoing harm and Down clocks. Everyone still able to react regains a reaction as the next round begins. The police now have a clear position and a reason to pursue. Jaz's next choice is whether to keep the case, collect Sal, or abandon the van.

### What to remember
The GM changed the scene through named people and visible risks. The dice handled uncertain actions. A single result did not also complete the mission, erase Heat, pay the crew or settle ownership of the case. Those happen when their own conditions are met.

### The next police move

At one star, officers first demand a stop. Close officers grab and cuff a susceptible or surrendered target. Tasers enter at two stars or against an armed person; CS and batons enter close up. At four stars, armed officers shoot. In a chase, nearby units PIT at low Heat and ram at three or more; armed units can fire at four. Officers get out within four squares of a suspect on foot.

Use Wheels d8 for an unassigned police driver, d6 for ordinary traffic, unless the vehicle has an explicit crew die. Add its actual Performance, Handling and ability modifiers. Traffic continues its own route at green pace. The standard mapped chase ends only when everyone wanted is **more than 30 squares** from every unit at round end.

# At the table · quick reference

### A check
Effort d6 + stat die + the **effective stat modifier** + other modifiers. Explode maximum faces. Set the TN before temporary boosts. A margin of 0 succeeds.

| Margin | Outcome | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| −4 or less | Proper Mess | Fails badly; serious consequence. |
| −3 to −1 | Bad Luck | Fails and costs you. |
| 0 | Just About | Success with a cost. |
| +1 to +3 | Sorted | Clean success. |
| +4 to +7 | Smashed It | Success and an extra benefit. |
| +8 or more | Legendary | An exceptional result. |

### Luck
1 Hustle steps Effort once. 2 Hustle gives On a Roll instead. One relevant perk steps the stat once. On a Roll: top two faces explode. Off Your Game: no explosions. Both together: normal. In Pain still stops Effort explosions.

| Outcome | Progress | Consequences |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Proper Mess | 0 | 2 |
| Bad Luck | 0 | 1 |
| Bad Luck, cost accepted | 1 | 1 |
| Just About | 1 | 1 |
| Sorted | 1 | 0 |
| Smashed It | 2 | 0 |
| Legendary | 3 | 0 |

\column

### A hit
Attack meets Guard → target chooses Take hit or reaction Dodge. Dodge is Moves against the attack total; meet it to avoid the hit. Damage is original attack margin + Impact − armour, minimum 0. One appropriate discretionary Condition; weapon effects also apply.

### Guard
People: **7 + best Moves/Grit modifier + worn armour Guard**, with Growth and situational changes. Motors: **6 + 2 × Size**. Light cover +2; heavy +4 and 1 reduction; enclosed rider +2; Prone −2; Grabbed −2; Stunned −2; Down −4.

### Ongoing harm
Bleeding 1 per round; Burning 2. Down: Grit Medium; Proper Mess or 3 rounds without aid → Wasted. First aid: Savvy Medium, +2 Health, remove Bleeding and Hurt, once per injury.

### A chase
Green to Speed. Yellow to 1½ × Speed: Medium, Handling −1. Red to 2 × Speed: Hard, Handling −2. Each action gets a new cumulative gauge. More than 30 squares from every unit at round end ends the chase; it does not itself clear Heat.

You start with a cracked phone, a few quid and a job under the arches. The next street has a better bike, a worse debt and somebody who swears they know you.

Work the borough. Keep the crew standing. Turn a favour into a relationship and a relationship into a way through the door. When the blue lights find you, decide what you are prepared to leave behind.

This rulebook contains the complete v0.5.0 play procedures, ten backgrounds, 42 perks, 31 Conditions, 171 items, 29 vehicles, four pregenerated characters and the 33-level Rep ladder.
